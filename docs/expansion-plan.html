Knights of the Eigenrealm — build plan

Eigenrealm Expansion

Four workstreams that move the game from a mechanically rich quiz over half a first course to something that genuinely teaches: more of the subject, the geometry drawn rather than described, problems that run several steps deep — and a fourth stream that keeps the first three from making the game worse at its job.

01

The four workstreams

A — Content

Reach the rest of the syllabus

Thirty-two new generators across three new realms, taking the game from eigenvalues to eigenvectors, from integrals to series, and finally to the gradient the Tome already promises.

B — Figures

Draw the geometry

A declarative figure spec on any generator, rendered beside the question — arrows, areas, tangents, transformed grids. The largest single gain in intuition, especially for linear algebra.

C — Chains

Teach procedure, not just recall

Boss fights pose a linked sequence — differentiate, solve, classify — where each step feeds the next and the finished chain reads back as a worked solution.

D — Learning

Keep fun and learning aligned

Remove the guess floor, teach before testing, and quarantine the spectacle away from the moment of thought. Carries the most learning weight of the four, and the least new content.

02

Where fun and learning diverge

Workstreams A–C came from asking what would make the game better. Workstream D came from asking whether that was the same question as what would make it teach better. It mostly isn't, and in three specific places the current build is working against itself.

The structural tension

Interesting-but-irrelevant graphics and sound reliably reduce learning from instructional material, because they compete for the working memory the maths needs. Screen shake during a determinant problem is load, not help. The entire "prettier and juicier" direction is textbook seductive detail.

The resolution is not to drop the spectacle — a tool you actually open beats a better tool you don't, and adherence is the real failure mode of self-study. The resolution is to separate them in time.

Phase of a turnTreatment
Question on screen, you are thinking Calm. No shake, no particles, no combo ticker, no countdown bar. Nothing moving but the idle breathing.
Answer submitted, resolution playing Everything. Hit-stop, flash, slow-motion, coins, banners, rising streak tones — the full reward, in the window where it costs nothing.

Three mechanics to fix, not add

  • The speed bonus rewards guessing. ×1.5 under seven seconds pushes pattern-matching over reasoning, and time pressure specifically degrades maths performance. Fix: apply it only to topics already mastered, where fluency is the honest goal, and switch it off on weak ones. The mastery model to do that already exists.
  • Easing weak topics removes the struggle. adjustDiff drops to tier 1 below 0.45 mastery. Comfort is where learning stops. Fix: hold weak topics at tier 2 and offer a hint instead.
  • The spacing is not spaced. dueIn counts questions, not days — so a topic revisited twelve questions later in the same sitting counts as spaced repetition. Fix: store a timestamp and schedule in days.
03

The shared spine

All four workstreams touch the same contract, so it changes once, first. A generator currently returns {topic, q, a, d, ex}.

FieldShapeRule
fig{kind, …} Drawn with the question. Must never reveal the answer — show u and v, never their sum.
figAnswer{kind, …} Drawn in the explanation, where giving it away is the point.
input{kind, slots, range} How the answer is built by tapping — number pad, sign-and-digit steppers, one slot per vector component or matrix entry.
CHAINS[key]{topic, intro, steps[], fig} Each step is a normal question; later steps may quote earlier answers.
example{worked, faded} Shown before a topic's first question, and again if mastery collapses.

Every field is optional, so the existing 38 generators keep working untouched on day one and gain the new behaviour as each is revisited.

05

Workstream B — figures

One renderer, six kinds, driven by data rather than per-question drawing code. Figures draw once to a static canvas — they must not join the battle's animation loop, which is the obvious way to leak frames.

KindDrawsServes
planeAxes, grid, labelled arrows, optional parallelogram, projection drop-line, angle arc Addition, scaling, combinations, dot product, length, orthogonality, projection, independence, cross product, gradients
gridThe unit square and a lattice, before and after a matrix acts on it Matrix × vector, determinant as area factor, transformations, eigenvectors as the arrows that keep their line
curveA function plotted over a range, with optional tangent line, marked points, asymptotes Limits, derivatives, tangents, critical points, concavity, L'Hôpital
regionShaded area between a curve and an axis, or between two curves Definite integrals, area under curves, average value, improper integrals
barsRiemann rectangles converging on a curve Riemann sums, the integral as a limit
numberlineA line with approach arrows from both sides and an open point Limits at a point, one-sided limits, 0/0 forms
The rule that makes or breaks this

A figure attached to the question must be answerable-from only by doing the maths. Drawing the resultant of a vector addition turns the problem into reading a picture. The payoff figure goes in figAnswer, where it becomes the explanation — and that split is worth enforcing with a test.

06

Workstream C — chains

Ten chains, each three or four steps, posed by bosses. Earlier answers stay on screen so the solution visibly assembles itself. A wrong step does not abort the chain — it costs the finisher.

ChainSteps
OptimisationDifferentiate → solve f′ = 0 → second-derivative test → report the extremum
Curve sketchingDifferentiate → critical points → classify each
Eigen-analysisCharacteristic polynomial → eigenvalues → an eigenvector
DiagonalisationEigenvalues → eigenvectors → assemble P and D
Linear systemAugmented matrix → row reduce → read the solution
Related ratesRelate the variables → differentiate in time → substitute
Definite integralAntiderivative → evaluate at bounds → subtract
Tangent lineDerivative → slope at the point → line equation
ProjectionDot product → norm squared → the projection vector
u-substitutionChoose u → find du → rewrite → integrate
  • Each correct step charges a finisher; completing the chain lands one large strike scaled by how many steps were clean.
  • The foe's wind-up meter is the open question — a three-step chain spans three turns, so a slam will land mid-chain. Freezing it reads fairer; letting it fire keeps the pressure. Decide before the chain UI is built.
  • Mastery records each step against its own topic, so a chain feeds the adaptive scheduler exactly like single questions.
07

Workstream D — learning

The least glamorous stream and the one with the most effect on whether any of this teaches you anything.

Retire the four options

Multiple choice tests recognition; recall requires generation, and the difference is large. A 25% guess floor also means the game cannot distinguish knows it from eliminated three — which quietly corrupts the mastery model every other feature depends on. Answers get built by tapping instead: a number pad for scalars, sign-and-digit steppers per component for vectors, one slot per entry for matrices. Minimal typing survives; the guess floor does not.

Teach before testing

There is currently no instruction anywhere in the game — it is pure examination. A topic's first appearance should show a fully worked example, then a faded one where you supply the last step, then unassisted problems. The same sequence returns if a topic's mastery collapses.

Make errors land

A wrong answer's explanation can be dismissed instantly. Requiring the correct answer to be re-entered before continuing forces the correction through, and costs nothing when you already understood it.

Ask why, occasionally

After some correct answers: which rule did you use? with three options. Self-explanation is cheap to build and disproportionately effective — and unlike the main question, recognition is the right format here.

The consequence nobody plans for

Removing multiple choice breaks two things that quietly depend on it. Sage's Insight — the relic that burns away two wrong answers — has nothing to burn, and needs redesigning into a hint that reveals the first step. And misconception tagging currently works by matching the chosen string against tagged distractors; with freely built answers it must instead compare the constructed value against a table of known-wrong values. Same data, different retrieval — but every generator is affected.

08

Phases

Ordered because the order is load-bearing: P0 unblocks everything, the cheap learning fixes land before content multiplies their effect, figures are proven on known-good problems before 32 new generators depend on them, and the UI cannot absorb 70 topics until P7.

P0spine

Contract and harness

Extend the generator contract with every optional field above, and — the real work — build a small expression evaluator so symbolic answers can be checked numerically. The current harness parses polynomials only, which will not survive series, implicit differentiation, or vector-valued answers.

Done whenThe existing 38 pass unchanged, and the harness can verify an arbitrary symbolic answer by evaluating it at sample points.
P1learning

The cheap fixes that pull the other way

Calm during the question and loud during resolution. Speed bonus restricted to mastered topics. Weak topics held at tier 2 with a hint rather than dropped to tier 1. Wrong answers require re-entering the right one. Spacing rescheduled in days instead of questions. Small, self-contained, and it improves the game you already have before a line of new content exists.

Done whenNothing animates while a question is unanswered; a topic answered today is scheduled for tomorrow, not twelve questions from now; and a full playthrough confirms the speed bonus never fires on a weak topic.
P2figures

Renderer, proven on content that already exists

All six figure kinds, attached to the current 38 generators. No new maths — this phase shakes the renderer out against known-good problems and delivers visible value immediately.

Done whenEvery kind renders at 320 px without overflow, no question figure reveals its answer, and a campaign playthrough leaks no canvases.
P3learning

Tap-to-build answers

The input widgets, the misconception lookup rewritten to match constructed values, Sage's Insight redesigned, and self-explanation prompts. The single largest learning change in the plan, and the one that most needs playing before it is extended to 70 topics.

Done whenEvery existing generator has an input spec, misconception feedback still fires on constructed answers, and campaign balance is re-simulated — accuracy will fall for everyone once guessing stops working.
P4content

Spectral Reach — advanced linear algebra

Twelve generators with grid and plane figures throughout. Eigenvectors drawn as the arrows that survive a transformation is the single most clarifying picture in the subject.

Done whenEach generator is numerically verified, tagging stays above 78%, and the realm's difficulty curve is simulated.
P5content

Infinite Expanse — advanced calculus

Thirteen generators leaning on curve, region and bars.

Done whenAs P4, plus every integral confirmed by quadrature and every derivative by finite differences.
P6content

Gradient Summit — the capstone

Seven generators where the two subjects finally meet: the gradient as a vector of partial derivatives, drawn on the plane renderer from P2. The Tome gains a closing page and the final boss becomes the game's thesis rather than just its hardest fight.

Done whenThe campaign is 32 fights, balance is re-simulated end to end, and a headless run clears all eight realms.
P7ui

Absorb 70 topics

The Chronicle and the Training picker both render one row per topic. At 70 that is a wall of bars and a wall of buttons. Both need grouping by strand with collapse, leading with the summary the Chronicle already computes.

Done whenBoth screens are navigable at 320 px with all 70 topics seen, and any topic is reachable in under two thumb-scrolls.
P8chains

Chains, worked examples and the boss rite

Ten chains, the step tracker, the finisher, the wind-up decision — and the worked-example ladder, which shares the same step machinery and so is cheapest to build here.

Done whenEvery chain completes, later steps follow from earlier ones, a new topic always opens with a worked example, and a boss fight is winnable and losable at plausible accuracies.

What this breaks

Found by reading the current code against the plan. The first two are live today, independent of anything here being built.

live Spacing is measured in questions, not days dueIn compares against qCount, so the spaced-repetition schedule has no notion that a week has passed. Inside one long session everything simply cycles. Fixed in P1.
live Dragonslayer fires three realms early Titles hardcodes cleared['4:3']. Add realms and the title awards for beating what is no longer the last boss. Must derive from REALMS.length, as Arena.unlocked() already correctly does.
P0 The verification harness cannot read the new answers Current numeric checks parse polynomial strings only. Series, implicit differentiation and vector-valued answers need a real expression evaluator, or the new content ships unverified — abandoning the one discipline that has caught every bug so far.
P3 Sage's Insight has nothing left to burn The relic removes two wrong options. With constructed answers there are no options. It becomes a hint that reveals the first step — a redesign, not a tweak.
P3 Every difficulty number becomes wrong at once Removing the 25% guess floor drops everyone's accuracy. The whole balance model — enemy health, wind-up damage, arena depth — was simulated against multiple-choice accuracy and must be re-derived.
P6 Arena floods with material you have never seen The arena pool is every topic, and unseen topics carry the highest selection weight so they are introduced promptly. At 70 topics that turns an early run into a wall of unfamiliar advanced problems. Gate the pool to unlocked realms.
P6 Enemy scaling runs out The curve was tuned for five realms and tops out near 92 attack. Three more need it extended and the campaign re-simulated, including the wind-up damage gear is balanced against.
P7 Boss cross-realm draws become trivia A boss reaches back into any earlier realm with equal probability. At realm eight the final boss can ask about vector addition. Weight the reach-back toward recent realms.
P8 The Scriptable file gets unwieldy to paste 140 KB today, an estimated 280 KB after this plan. Well inside what Scriptable runs, but large enough that the build should start minifying the embedded HTML.
10

Decisions I need from you

01 How far does tap-to-build go? Every question, or multiple choice on a topic's first exposures and construction once you have seen it — scaffolding that fades as mastery rises. 02 Does the foe's wind-up fire mid-chain, or freeze until the chain resolves? Freezing reads fairer; firing keeps the pressure that makes gear matter. 03 Chains in Training as well as boss fights? They are the best teaching tool in the plan, and locking them to bosses hides them behind the campaign. 04 Ship realm by realm, or hold everything for one release? 05 Is 70 topics the right ceiling, or would you rather go deeper on fewer — more problem variety per topic instead of more topics?
11

What I would do first

P1 on its own, then stop and let you play. It adds no content, no art and no new mechanics — it fixes three things that are quietly working against you, moves the spectacle out of the thinking window, and makes the spaced repetition actually spaced. It is the smallest change in the plan with the largest effect on whether the hours you put in stick.

Then P2 and P3 together: the geometry drawn, and the guessing removed. That pairing is where the game stops being a quiz. Content waves are the larger prize but the least urgent — thirty-two more topics taught the current way is thirty-two more topics you can guess your way through.
