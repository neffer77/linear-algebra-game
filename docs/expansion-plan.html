Knights of the Eigenrealm — build plan

Eigenrealm Expansion

Fun is the top priority. The teaching rides along inside it — complex ideas broken into five-second pieces that chain into combos, and tooltips you can peek at without leaving the fight. Nothing here asks you to sit through a lesson.

01

The principle

Earlier drafts of this plan treated fun and learning as a tradeoff to be managed. That framing is wrong for this game, and the fix is structural rather than a compromise: decompose complex topics into pieces small enough to be fun, and the decomposition is the teaching.

Four short steps that each land in five seconds, chained into a combo with a finisher, is a better game moment than one hard question — faster, more rhythmic, more wins per minute. It also happens to be how procedural fluency is actually built. The two goals stop fighting once the unit of play gets small enough.

The rule this plan follows

Every teaching mechanic must earn its place as a game mechanic first. Scaffolding fades because the reward for going unassisted grows. Hints exist because a peek costs something. Nothing is added because it is pedagogically virtuous.

02

The four workstreams

A — Look

Stop looking like a prototype

Shaded sprites with rim light, a distinct sky and weather per realm, a heraldic UI, and an embedded display face. The fastest visible change in the plan.

B — Reward

Give the wins ceremony

Rarity tiers and a chest that opens, bounties, a two-minute daily skirmish, a visible next unlock, and near-death tension.

C — Motes

Micro-learning as a combo system

Complex topics split into five-second steps that chain, with tooltips one tap away and scaffolding that fades into bigger damage.

D — Content

Reach the rest of the syllabus

Thirty-two new generators across three new realms, each complex one shipping with its mote ladder from day one.

Diagnosed against the current build, worst offender first.

04

Workstream B — reward

  • Loot needs a reveal. Today a 42% roll picks one of four consumables and prints a line of text. It should be rarity tiers with colour and a shine, a chest that shakes before it opens, a NEW stamp, and rare drops that are genuinely rare and genuinely good. The single biggest missing beat.
  • A two-minute mode. There is nothing you can play in a queue. A daily skirmish — fixed seed, one run a day, personal best kept — is what turns this from a project into a habit.
  • Bounties. Three rotating goals: land ten dot products, win a fight untouched, reach a streak of twelve. Cheap to build, and they give a session a reason beyond the next node.
  • Enemies that do things. All twenty foes are identical but for health and attack. A shielded foe needing two correct answers to break through, a mimic that steals gold on a miss, a swarm that splits — that is what makes a fight memorable rather than a stat check.
  • Near-death tension. Below a quarter health: a red vignette pulsing at heartbeat tempo, the audio muffling. Costs almost nothing, and it is the most visceral feedback a game has.
  • Always show the next unlock. "Vector Saber — 150 gold, you have 96." A visible next thing is why anyone plays one more fight.
05

Workstream C — motes and tooltips

A mote is one atomic step of a complex topic, answerable in about five seconds. Every generator declares a complexity of 1 to 3; anything at 3 must ship with a mote ladder. Roughly fourteen topics qualify, giving about forty motes.

How a ladder plays

A complex foe forces a Rite: the motes come in sequence, each a quick tap, each landing its own small hit and rising tone, ending in one large finisher scaled by how many were clean. It reads as a combo string, not a lesson.

pick u→ find du→ rewrite→ integrate→ FINISHER ×3
Scaffolding fade as a power curve

Clear a topic's ladder a few times and the game offers the whole problem in one shot for far more damage. The support disappears because you chose the bigger reward, not because a difficulty setting moved. Learning progress is the power curve.

Tooltips — the Codex

  • Every topic carries a card: one sentence of rule, one tiny example, one figure. Never more than about twenty-five words of prose.
  • A ? chip sits beside the topic label. Free to peek on your first few encounters with a topic; after that a peek costs combo, not health — so it stays cheap enough to use and expensive enough to matter.
  • Jargon inside a question is tappable: determinant, orthogonal, antiderivative each pop a one-line definition without leaving the fight.
  • Get one wrong and the relevant card opens by itself, alongside the existing misconception diagnosis.

Perfect Strike — generation, opt-in

Four options mean a 25% guess floor, which is fast and fun but cannot tell knew it from eliminated three. Rather than forcing constructed answers on everyone, any question can be answered with a Perfect Strike: tap to build the answer yourself instead of choosing, for roughly triple damage, with a miss costing the combo. Risk and reward, and the mastery model gets clean signal from the players who take it.

Figures come along for the ride: six data-driven kinds — plane, grid, curve, region, bars, numberline — drawn once to a static canvas. The rule that matters: a question figure must never reveal its answer. Draw u and v, never their sum; the payoff picture belongs in the explanation.

07

Three fixes that cost no fun

Small, invisible, and they stop the current build working against itself.

  • Calm during the question, loud on resolution. Nothing moves while you are thinking; the full hit-stop, flash, slow-motion and coin shower fire the instant you answer. Keeps every bit of the spectacle and takes it out of the one window where it competes with the maths.
  • Widen the speed windows. Seven seconds for the ×1.5 rewards snap judgement over reasoning. Scale the window with difficulty — roughly twelve and twenty-five seconds at the top tier — so careful thought still earns the bonus. The fun of a speed reward survives; the pressure to guess does not.
  • Make the spacing actually spaced. dueIn currently counts questions, not days, so a topic revisited twelve questions later in the same sitting counts as spaced repetition. Store a timestamp and schedule in days.
08

Phases

Fun-first ordering: the look and the reward loop land before any new mathematics, because they are what make the reps happen at all.

P0look

Sprites, skies and weather

Shaded sprites with rim light and contact shadows, then a distinct composition and weather system per realm with two parallax layers. No new mechanics — this is the change you see in the first two seconds.

Done whenNo two realms share a skyline, weather runs inside the existing frame budget on a mid phone, and reduced-motion still disables it all.
P1look

Heraldic UI and a display face

Banner headers, gold rules, shield-shaped question card, one inlined display face, and screen transitions with weight.

Done whenThe six device widths still pass the overflow and tap-target audit, and the Scriptable build still runs offline.
P2reward

Loot ceremony, bounties, tension

Rarity tiers and the chest reveal, three rotating bounties, the next-unlock tracker, low-health vignette and heartbeat, and the two-minute daily skirmish.

Done whenA drop is never just text, a session always has a visible next goal, and the daily runs from a fixed seed so a score means something.
P3spine

Contract, motes and harness

Extend the generator contract with complexity, motes[], codex, fig and input. Build the expression evaluator the verification harness needs — it currently parses polynomials only, which will not survive series or vector-valued answers.

Done whenThe existing 38 pass unchanged and the harness can verify an arbitrary symbolic answer numerically.
P4motes

The Rite, the Codex and Perfect Strike

Mote ladders on the fourteen complex topics that already exist, the combo and finisher, the tooltip system, scaffolding fade as a damage upgrade, and opt-in constructed answers. Also the three no-cost fixes from section 07.

Done whenEvery complex topic has a ladder and a card, a ladder is quicker to clear than the single question it replaces, and the fade triggers on mastery rather than on a timer.
P5figures

Figure renderer on existing content

All six kinds, attached to the current 38 generators. Proven against known-good problems before 32 new ones depend on it.

Done whenEvery kind renders at 320 px without overflow, no question figure reveals its answer, and a campaign playthrough leaks no canvases.
P6content

Spectral Reach, then Infinite Expanse

Twenty-five generators across two realms, each complex one arriving with its mote ladder, Codex card and figures.

Done whenEach is numerically verified, misconception tagging stays above 78%, and each realm's curve is simulated.
P7content

Gradient Summit, and absorbing 70 topics

The capstone realm where the two subjects meet, plus the UI work 70 topics forces: the Chronicle and Training picker both render one row per topic, which at 70 is a wall of bars and a wall of buttons.

Done whenThe campaign is 32 fights, balance is re-simulated end to end, and any topic is reachable in under two thumb-scrolls at 320 px.
09

What this breaks

Found by reading the current code against the plan. The first two are live today.

live Spacing is measured in questions, not days dueIn compares against qCount, so the schedule has no notion that a week has passed. Fixed in P4.
live Dragonslayer fires three realms early Titles hardcodes cleared['4:3']. Add realms and the title awards for beating what is no longer the last boss. Must derive from REALMS.length, as Arena.unlocked() already does.
P0 Weather is a frame budget risk Per-realm particle systems run every frame alongside the existing animation loop, inside a WebView on a phone. Needs a hard particle cap and to be first thing dropped when reduced motion is on.
P4 Motes distort the mastery model Four easy motes recorded as four correct answers will inflate a topic's mastery far faster than one hard question would. Motes must record against the parent topic with reduced weight, or the adaptive scheduler will believe you have mastered things you have only been walked through.
P4 Sage's Insight overlaps the Codex The relic burns two wrong options; the tooltip gives the rule. With both in play the relic is redundant on easy questions and useless during a mote ladder. Rework it into a mote skip, or fold it into free Codex peeks.
P4 Perfect Strike breaks the balance model Triple damage on constructed answers changes every fight length. The campaign and arena curves were simulated against a fixed damage model and need re-deriving with an opt-in multiplier in play.
P7 Arena floods with unseen material The arena pool is every topic, and unseen topics carry the highest selection weight. At 70 topics an early run becomes a wall of unfamiliar advanced problems. Gate the pool to unlocked realms.
P7 Enemy scaling runs out Tuned for five realms, topping out near 92 attack. Three more need the curve extended and the campaign re-simulated.
P7 The Scriptable file gets unwieldy 140 KB today; an inlined display face plus this content puts it near 320 KB. Still runs, but the build should start minifying.
10

Decisions I need from you

01 Do mote ladders fire automatically on complex topics, or only when you choose them — the safe route in exchange for a smaller finisher? 02 Does the foe's wind-up fire mid-ladder, or freeze until the Rite resolves? Freezing reads fairer; firing keeps the pressure that makes gear matter. 03 Should a Codex peek cost combo, cost nothing, or cost nothing the first few times and combo after? 04 Ship realm by realm, or hold everything for one release? 05 Is 70 topics the right ceiling, or would you rather go deeper on fewer — more problem variety per topic instead of more topics?
11

What I would do first

P0 and P2 together — the look and the loot ceremony. Between them they change the first two seconds and the best moment of every fight, and neither touches a line of mathematics. That is the fastest route to a game you want to keep opening, which is the precondition for everything else in this plan mattering.

Then P3 and P4, where the motes and the Codex arrive and the game starts teaching in pieces small enough to enjoy. Content last: thirty-two more topics are worth far more once there is a good way to learn each one.
