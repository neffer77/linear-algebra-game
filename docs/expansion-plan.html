Knights of the Eigenrealm — build plan

Eigenrealm Expansion

Three workstreams that move the game from a mechanically rich quiz over half a first course, to something that actually teaches linear algebra and calculus: more of the subject, the geometry drawn rather than described, and problems that run several steps deep.

01

The three workstreams

A — Content

Reach the rest of the syllabus

Thirty-two new generators across three new realms, taking the game from eigenvalues to eigenvectors, from integrals to series, and finally to the gradient the Tome already promises.

B — Figures

Draw the geometry

A declarative figure spec on any generator, rendered to a small canvas beside the question — arrows, areas, tangents, transformed grids. The largest single gain in intuition, especially for linear algebra.

C — Chains

Teach procedure, not just recall

Boss fights pose a linked sequence — differentiate, solve, classify — where each step feeds the next and the completed chain reads back as a worked solution.

02

The shared spine

All three workstreams touch the same contract, so it changes once, first. A generator currently returns {topic, q, a, d, ex}. It gains two optional fields, and a parallel collection appears for chains.

FieldShapeRule
fig{kind, …} Drawn with the question. Must never reveal the answer — show u and v, never their sum.
figAnswer{kind, …} Drawn in the explanation, where giving it away is the point.
CHAINS[key]{topic, intro, steps[], fig} Each step is a normal {q, a, d, ex}; later steps may quote earlier answers.

Everything downstream — buildQuestion, Mastery, Battle, Training, the Chronicle — keeps working on generators that omit the new fields, so the change is additive and the existing 38 need no edits on day one.

03

Workstream A — the missing syllabus

Three new realms, each a coherent strand rather than a grab bag. The eighth is the capstone the game has been pointing at from the beginning: the Tome's last page already says gradients are where the two subjects meet.

Every new generator carries the same obligations as the existing ones: three difficulty tiers, four unique choices, a worked explanation, and distractors tagged with the specific misconception they encode — the tagging rate must not fall below the current 78%.

04

Workstream B — figures

One renderer, six kinds, driven by data rather than per-question drawing code. Figures draw once to a static canvas — they must not join the battle's animation loop, which is the obvious way to leak frames.

KindDrawsServes
planeAxes, grid, labelled arrows, optional parallelogram, projection drop-line, angle arc Addition, scaling, combinations, dot product, length, orthogonality, projection, independence, cross product
gridThe unit square and a lattice, before and after a matrix acts on it Matrix × vector, determinant as area factor, transformations, eigenvectors as the arrows that keep their line
curveA function plotted over a range, with optional tangent line, marked points, asymptotes Limits, derivatives, tangents, critical points, concavity, L'Hôpital
regionShaded area between a curve and an axis, or between two curves Definite integrals, area under curves, average value, improper integrals
barsRiemann rectangles converging on a curve Riemann sums, the integral as a limit
numberlineA line with approach arrows from both sides and an open point Limits at a point, one-sided limits, 0/0 forms
The rule that makes or breaks this

A figure attached to the question must be answerable-from only by doing the maths. Drawing the resultant of a vector addition turns the problem into reading a picture. The payoff figure goes in figAnswer, where it becomes the explanation — and that split is worth enforcing with a test.

Roughly 30 of the eventual 70 generators can carry a question figure; nearly all can carry an answer figure. Figures inherit the game's palette and must stay legible at 320 px wide.

05

Workstream C — chains

Ten chains, each three or four steps, posed by bosses. The player answers steps in sequence; earlier answers stay on screen so the solution visibly assembles itself. Getting a step wrong does not abort the chain — it costs the finisher.

ChainSteps
OptimisationDifferentiate → solve f′ = 0 → second-derivative test → report the extremum
Curve sketchingDifferentiate → critical points → classify each
Eigen-analysisCharacteristic polynomial → eigenvalues → an eigenvector
DiagonalisationEigenvalues → eigenvectors → assemble P and D
Linear systemAugmented matrix → row reduce → read the solution
Related ratesRelate the variables → differentiate in time → substitute
Definite integralAntiderivative → evaluate at bounds → subtract
Tangent lineDerivative → slope at the point → line equation
ProjectionDot product → norm squared → the projection vector
u-substitutionChoose u → find du → rewrite → integrate

Combat integration

  • Each correct step charges a finisher; completing the chain lands one large strike scaled by how many steps were clean. This makes a boss feel different from a rank-and-file foe without adding a new interaction.
  • The foe's wind-up meter is the open question — a three-step chain spans three turns, so the slam will land mid-chain. Freezing it during a chain is simpler and reads fairer; letting it fire keeps the pressure. This needs deciding before the chain UI is built.
  • Mastery records each step against its own topic, so a chain feeds the adaptive scheduler exactly like single questions.
06

Phases

Ordered because the order is load-bearing: P0 unblocks everything, figures on existing content prove the renderer before 32 new generators depend on it, and the UI cannot absorb 70 topics until P5.

P0spine

Contract and harness

Extend the generator contract with fig / figAnswer, add the CHAINS collection and its battle mode behind a flag, and — the real work — build a small expression evaluator so symbolic answers can be checked numerically. The current harness only parses polynomials, which will not survive series, implicit differentiation, or vector-valued answers.

Done whenThe existing 38 generators pass unchanged, and the new harness can verify an arbitrary symbolic answer by evaluating it at sample points.
P1figures

Renderer, proven on content that already exists

Build all six figure kinds and attach them to the current 38 generators. No new maths — this phase exists to shake the renderer out against known-good problems and to deliver visible value immediately.

Done whenEvery figure kind renders at 320 px without overflow, no question figure reveals its answer, and a full campaign playthrough leaks no canvases.
P2content

Spectral Reach — advanced linear algebra

Twelve generators, realm six, with grid and plane figures throughout. Eigenvectors drawn as the arrows that survive a transformation is the single most clarifying picture in the subject.

Done whenEach generator is numerically verified, tagging stays above 78%, and the realm's difficulty curve is simulated.
P3content

Infinite Expanse — advanced calculus

Thirteen generators, realm seven, leaning on curve, region and bars.

Done whenAs P2, plus every integral answer is confirmed by quadrature and every derivative by finite differences.
P4content

Gradient Summit — the capstone

Seven generators, realm eight, where the two subjects finally meet: the gradient as a vector of partial derivatives, drawn on the plane renderer built in P1. The Tome gains a closing page and the final boss becomes the game's thesis rather than just its hardest fight.

Done whenThe campaign is 32 fights, balance is re-simulated end to end, and a headless run clears all eight realms.
P5ui

Absorb 70 topics

The Chronicle and the Training picker both render one row per topic. At 70 that is a wall of bars and a wall of buttons. Both need grouping by strand with collapse, and the Chronicle should lead with the summary it already computes.

Done whenBoth screens are navigable at 320 px with all 70 topics seen, and no screen exceeds two thumb-scrolls to reach any topic.
P6chains

Chains and the boss rite

Ten chains, the step tracker, the finisher, and the wind-up decision. Last because chains are the only workstream that changes the shape of a turn, and it is cheaper to change that once the content it draws on exists.

Done whenEvery chain completes, each step's answer is among its choices, later steps follow from earlier ones, and a boss fight is winnable and losable at plausible accuracies.
07

What this breaks

Found by reading the current code against the plan. The first one is a live bug the moment a sixth realm exists.

P0 Dragonslayer fires three realms early Titles hardcodes cleared['4:3']. Add realms and the title awards for beating what is no longer the last boss. Must derive from REALMS.length, as Arena.unlocked() already correctly does.
P0 The verification harness cannot read the new answers Current numeric checks parse polynomial strings only. Series, implicit differentiation, vector-valued and set-valued answers all need a real expression evaluator, or the new content ships unverified — which would abandon the one discipline that has caught every bug so far.
P4 Arena floods with material you have never seen The arena pool is every topic, and unseen topics carry the highest selection weight so they get introduced promptly. At 70 topics that turns an early arena run into a wall of unfamiliar advanced problems. Gate the pool to topics whose realm is unlocked.
P4 Enemy scaling runs out The curve was tuned for five realms and tops out around 92 attack. Three more realms need the curve extended and the whole campaign re-simulated, including the wind-up damage that gear is balanced against.
P5 Boss cross-realm draws become trivia A boss reaches back into any earlier realm with equal probability. At realm eight that means the final boss can ask about vector addition. Weight the reach-back toward recent realms.
P6 The Scriptable file gets unwieldy to paste 140 KB today, an estimated 260 KB after this plan. Still well inside what Scriptable runs, but large enough that the build should probably start minifying the embedded HTML.
P6 Grand Magister becomes a different promise Mastering every topic goes from 38 to 70. That is intentional — but it is worth a second, reachable title so the ladder does not end in a cliff.
08

Decisions I need from you

01 Does the foe's wind-up fire mid-chain, or freeze until the chain resolves? Freezing reads fairer; firing keeps the pressure that makes gear matter. 02 Chains in Training as well as boss fights? They are the best teaching tool in the plan, and locking them to bosses hides them behind the campaign. 03 Ship realm by realm, or hold everything for one release? Incremental means you can play P2 while P3 is built. 04 Is 70 topics the right ceiling, or would you rather go deeper on fewer — more problem variety per topic instead of more topics?
09

What I would do first

P0 and P1 together, then stop and let you play. That combination adds no new mathematics but changes how the existing game feels to learn from — every vector becomes an arrow, every integral a shaded region, every tangent a line touching a curve. It is the cheapest way to find out whether drawing the geometry actually helps you before committing to 32 generators that assume it does.

The content waves are the larger prize, but they are also the part most improved by knowing how you actually play.
