Knights of the Eigenrealm · build audit

Twenty skills were designed.
All twenty are in the game.

The Skill Web placed all twenty and gave each a setting, a branch of mathematics, and a way of paying the player back. This is what actually shipped, measured against the code rather than against memory — and after twelve slices and a reordering, the answer is all of it. The roster went from one to twenty, all four ways a skill can pay are filled, every setting the design named is built, and nothing on the roll is half. This page has spent five revisions as a list of what was missing. There is nothing left on it, so what it is for now is the record of how that happened, including the parts that went wrong.

The muster, by how a skill pays you back

The Skill Web's central idea is that a skill pays through one of four channels — and a skill missing from the economy simply pays through a different one. Grouping the roll that way shows the shape of the gap rather than just its size.

Foresight is complete, and the five are deliberately different questions rather than one answer at five strengths. Farsight reads the next room exactly. Rumours totals the next three and says nothing about their order. Sighting gives two distances and no detail at all — how far to the next champion, how far to the next room that pays. Tracking answers none of those and instead reads a shape: the period the danger moves on, where in that turn you are standing, and therefore whether the stretch ahead worsens or eases. And Weather-reading is the only one that reads something drawn rather than derived — the swell has a period a clever player could work out unaided, but which leagues run rough is a coin the sea has already flipped, so a forecast is the only way anyone can know it.

Where the skills were meant to live

Each skill is learned in a setting, and the settings are what create the pull to leave a place you like. All eight now exist: the Deep you descend, the Keep where ore, essence, herbs and pages are spent, the Sanctum, which pays essence rather than gold, the Tavern, which pays gold at variance and is the only place that can send you home poorer than you arrived, the Summit, which is climbed and whose danger is not its foes but the air, the Wilds, which is walked across and whose danger circles rather than climbs, the Sea, whose danger is drawn — each league is a coin the weather has already flipped — and the Library, which is the only place where the pressure is the questions themselves: every few shelves the riddles get harder, and no amount of health saves you from that.

Every skill on the roll now has the place it names. Four of them were homeless three slices ago and shipped anyway, which was deliberate rather than a shortcut: charges and tiers are cut from the mathematics a skill draws on, never from admission, and the Sanctum's own suite asserted that for Scrying before any of them shipped. It is worth saying what that bought — because the skills went first, the Summit, the Wilds, the Sea and the Library each arrived with the ability that answers it already written and already tested, and closing a setting cost a room rather than a system.

How the gap got smaller

The roadmap first built here — P0 through P6 — was scoped as a vertical slice, and says so in its own text. It bought depth and deliberately deferred breadth, which is why the first version of this page counted one skill. The mismatch between “finish the build order” and “get the twenty skills” should have been named at the start rather than found by playing the result.

Twelve slices and a reordering closed it, in the order that made each one cheapest:

S1

The loadout frame

shipped

Three slots, charges read off mastery in Dial Three's bands — weak none, shaky one, steady two, solid three — filling the power bar that already existed. No ability may reduce the number of questions in a fight, and the suite fires each one mid-fight and asserts the foe's health does not move, so a future ability that breaks the rule fails the build rather than shipping.

S2

The Deep–Keep chain

shipped

Delving cuts ore from seams; Smithing spends it on picks and on gear no coin buys; Lockpicking opens chests for essence; Enchanting spends essence on permanent runes. A closed loop inside two settings, with the Skill Web's own warning asserted rather than assumed: multipliers, never gates. A knight with nothing can still descend, and a chest met without a pick is a dead end rather than a dead run.

S3

Scrying, and the Sanctum

shipped

The first ability spent outside a fight, on the decision itself, with charges that last a descent rather than a room. The harness showed why that scarcity is the whole balance: simulated with unlimited scrying, a good player bails out of 97% of runs and the fork becomes an algorithm. The Sanctum is the second real setting and the pattern the remaining five will follow — its own curve, room plan, mathematics and material.

Two bugs of the same shape turned up on the way, and both were shipped before they were found: the fork and the death screen each described the room you had just left by reaching for its foe, so clearing a seam crashed them. The room contract now walks every kind through the fork in its own suite.

S4

The Tavern, Dice and Cards

shipped

The two skills the game had been carrying unnamed since the beginning — the Berserker Rune was always a bet, the Sage's Insight was always information — became skills, so the uses come off mastery instead of a shop. Their setting is the first that cannot kill you and can still send you home poorer: you buy in at the door and every table plays for the pot. Three of its five rooms are hands, and a hand is the only room where the decision comes before the question.

S5

Passage — the empty channel, filled

shipped

Four skills of a shape nothing else in the game has. They are not carried, have no charges, and cannot be pressed: two tiers each, earned at steady and solid, true everywhere until the strand goes cold. Climbing opens a route onto ground you have banked from; Dead reckoning makes a fall cost less than everything; the beast takes the opening blows; Loremaster slows the forgetting curve for every topic at once.

Loremaster is the only effect that reaches back into the mastery model rather than reading out of it, which makes it circular — it is priced off faded mastery of its own strand and is the thing deciding how fast that fades. Left alone it does not merely inflate: it recurses until the stack goes. A guard prices it plainly, so the skill is judged on what you actually know rather than on what it has already done for you.

S6

Rumours, and a table that sells them

shipped

The second foresight, built to answer a question the first does not: three rooms counted and totalled, silent about their order, and lasting the stretch it describes rather than being spent on the next room. The Tavern gained a table that sells you what the remaining hands will ask about, for a cut of the pot rather than a fixed price — so information costs what it is worth to the person buying it, and folding is free.

S7

The Summit, and Sighting

shipped

The fifth setting, climbed rather than descended, and the first whose danger is not its foes. Every room above the first takes a slice off the health a knight can hold, so what ends a climb is your own shrinking capacity. How high you got is what Climbing reads next time, which gave that skill the home it had been naming since it shipped without one. Sighting is the third foresight: two distances, no detail.

M1

The map, reordered

shipped

The open question below, answered. Places you can go now sit above the campaign rather than below it, sealed ones collapse to a single line, and the five hand-written node blocks became a table — so the three settings still to come are a line each. Nothing tested the order before, which is precisely why it could drift that far without anything going red.

S8

The ward-stone

shipped

The last half-built skill, finished — and not by a setting, which is what made it the awkward one. Spellcraft got a room instead: a stone you cut a ward into, one layer per clean answer, each layer halving one blow for the rest of the descent. Two things make it practice rather than another room. Its riddles are filtered to the skill's own strand rather than merely preferred, because a place that teaches Spellcraft two riddles in three is not where Spellcraft is practised — and it reads that strand off the ability table, so re-pointing Ward re-points the stone. What a layer is worth is fixed by where it sits in the cascade: the beast first because it is free, then a raised Ward because the player pressed it for this blow, then a banked layer because it will still be there for the next one.

S9

The Wilds, and Tracking

shipped

The sixth setting, and the first whose danger does not only grow. A herd circles it, so the curve carries a swell — nine rooms crest to crest, about a third either side of the middle. It is the only place where a room deeper in can be softer than the one behind it, and that changes what the fork asks: not "can I take the next room", which Farsight already answers, but "is the stretch ahead worsening or easing", which no one-room reading can. Hence Tracking, the fourth foresight and the first that reads a shape rather than a place. It lasts a whole turn of the swell, is recomputed at every fork rather than stored, and has a place where it has nothing to say — in a setting that only climbs there is no period to read, so the fork says so and keeps the charge.

S10

Herbalism, and a third currency

shipped

The Wilds shipped without a material and said so in its own table; this closed it. The thicket is the first room in the game with two decisions in it: riddles buy basket space, and the space is then spent on five plants that differ in worth and in bulk. Knowing the mathematics gets you a bigger basket and tells you nothing about how to fill it, so a knight who answers everything and packs badly walks out with less than one who answered less and chose well. The Apothecary at the Keep spends what it gathers, including one permanent — a satchel that makes every draught go half again as far — that gold cannot buy at any price.

S11a

The Sea, and a hold that compounds

shipped

The seventh setting, and the third kind of pressure: the Deep steepens, the Wilds circles, and the Sea draws. A league is rough or calm on its own coin, and a rough one hits half again as hard. That is the only pressure in the game a player cannot deduce from the outside, which is what Weather-reading is for — and it is why the squall had to be drawn on its own stream rather than off the room's, or reading the sky would have quietly changed the sea it was reading. Cargo is the setting's material and the one thing in the game you can carry that grows: it compounds 12% a league and goes down with the ship, so it is priced against exactly what Dead reckoning would have saved. The first version compounded in the room rather than on the league cleared, which made stepping out and back in free money; growth now happens once, on leagues actually behind you.

S11b

The Library, and the last kind of danger

shipped

The eighth and final setting, whose pressure is not health at all: every few shelves the questions themselves get harder, and nothing a knight carries answers that. It is therefore the one setting that names a counter rather than a foresight — Loremaster, a Passage skill, buys shelves between steps rather than sight — and the roll now asserts that a setting takes one or the other and never both. Its room is the scriptorium, where a slip does not merely cost you a line: precision falls, and every line after it is worth less for the rest of the page. Pages buy recency and never mastery, which is asserted field by field, because a bench that could sell you knowing the mathematics would undo the thing the whole game is built on.

S12

Alchemy, and a choice that had to be found twice

shipped

The last entry, and the only skill that converts — the third verb the Material channel names and the one nothing in the game did. It has no gathering room, for the same reason Smithing and Enchanting have none: there is nothing to go and cut. What it needed was a bench and a rate, and the rate is a diffusion rather than a price list. Flux runs down a gradient, the flow flattens the gradient, and no ceiling reaches 1 — so a round trip strictly loses and the bench prices the pull between settings instead of deleting it.

It shipped that way, and then someone asked what the choice at the bench actually was. Measured rather than defended, the answer was: thinner than it should be. The dominant play was always to pour the least that clears the line you want and come back for the rest, and splitting cost nothing — 80 poured at once and two lots of 40 come to the same number, because the integration carries its own state. The amount was never a commitment. One pour per descent made it one: over-pour and the material is gone for this trip, under-pour and the thing you wanted is still out of reach when you get back. That is the shape of the seam's depth, the thicket's basket and the hold's share, and it was missing here.

what that cost A second commit, a codec bump to v7 for one bit of charge, and seven suites that had deliberately pinned the old format version so a bump would make someone go and look. They did. Cheaper than shipping a bench whose only decision was arithmetic.

Three findings from the balance harness are worth keeping. The Summit as first written had no top: thin air asymptotes, so once it reached its floor a strong player climbed forever, and the foe curve had to keep growing past that point to give the climb a summit. And Farsight is worth almost nothing up there — correctly, because it reads one foe and the Summit kills by attrition. Settings now declare which foresight they are built around, and the harness asserts that property only where it applies. The third is that the harness held its own copy of the room layout, with a comment warning that a room kind added to the game and not to it would leave the sweep measuring a game nobody was playing — a third copy of a cascade that already existed twice. It is one function now, called by the shell when it builds a room and by foresight when it reads one. Ward-stones, added to it, move the Summit's break-even from 11/12/14/17/22 to 11/12/16/19/23: deeper, still bounded, still rising with skill.

And then it happened anyway, one slice later, which is the more useful half of the story. The Wilds shipped and was swept; thickets landed in the next slice and the sweep came back byte-identical, although a third of the setting's rooms had stopped being fights. The harness reads the shared cascade now but still needed a branch for the new kind, and had none — so it had quietly gone on measuring a place nobody was playing. The Wilds' break-even is 10/12/18/20/27; the 9/11/13/19/26 published here a slice ago was wrong. A single source of truth for the LAYOUT does not give you one for what each room COSTS.

The Library made the same point a third way, and the loudest. Its first sweep came back with no top: at 85% and 95% the break-even ran clean off depth forty, which is the signature of a setting with no pressure in it. The harness was measuring the gentlest foe curve in the game and nothing else, because the thing that actually ends a run in the stacks is the questions getting harder, and a sweep that models a knight's accuracy as a constant cannot see that. It now spends accuracy per tier — the one number on this page that is an assumption rather than a reading, and labelled as such in the harness. With it, the stacks have an end: 13 / 16 / 21 / 24 / 27. The Sea reads 10 / 14 / 17 / 22 / 28, and the two settings sit at opposite ends of what a one-room reading is worth: on the Sea a strong knight with Farsight bails out of half the runs they would otherwise have sailed, because the next league is a coin and seeing it flip is the whole game — in the Library the same knight bails out of none of them, because the thing killing them is four shelves away and no reading of the next room can see it.

The roll is closed

There is nothing left to list. Every skill the Skill Web named exists, every setting is built, all four payoff channels are full, and no entry is half. The gate this page set for the last slice held: nothing added since has become a way to buy mastery. Pages buy recency and the Crucible buys conversion, and both are asserted field by field against the mastery model rather than trusted.

The one that was not a place

done

This slot held Spellcraft through four revisions of this page: the one entry on the roll that a new setting would not fix, and the one most likely to stay half forever unless it was named as work. It was named, and the ward-stone closed it — which is the argument for keeping a list like this one honest rather than tidy. Nothing is left in this column.

The open question this page carried from its first version — whether the map should be reordered — is closed, and the last two settings proved it was worth doing: the Sea and the Library each landed as one line in a table, in the right place, with nothing to rearrange. Before that reorder they would have been appended below eight campaign realms and thirty-two fights, thirty-three nodes in front of the Deep.

Everything above is live. All of it is on main and deployed, and the full suite runs in CI on every merge — including five balance sweeps, one for each setting that never ends, because a place shipped unmeasured is exactly how the Deep once came to be unplayable. S11 took two attempts to get there, for a reason worth noting: the first run died before it reached the deploy, not on the game but on an apt mirror handing the runner a bad package hash while it installed a browser. Every check that ran, passed — the ones that mattered never got to run at all. That step is now cached and retried, and the retry clears the stale package index that causes the fault rather than simply hoping twice. It did not need to fire for this slice, which went green first time — but the caching is not what would have saved S11, and it is worth being exact about that: a cache hit skips the browser download, not the package-index update that actually broke.
